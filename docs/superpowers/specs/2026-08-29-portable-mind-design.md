## Premise

The blog opened in 2024-03-12 ("Ideas about definition of mindset") with five cognitive properties of engineering judgment — Simulation, Abstraction, Rationality, Awareness, Optimization — plus a precondition, Noticing, that sits outside all five. That post treated the properties narratively, with a fresh illustrative scenario per property and no formal proof apparatus (the site's formal Definition/Proposition/KaTeX conventions came later). This series returns to formalize what the founding post only sketched, and asks the question the blog's own arc has been circling since: do these five properties, properly formalized, apply to the AI agents this blog now spends most of its time on.

**Frontier counterpart, real and dated**: Han, Andreas, Fedorenko & de Varda, "Modular Cognitive Architecture Emerges in Large Language Models" (submitted 27 June 2026). Across 46 tasks spanning language, formal reasoning, social reasoning, and physical reasoning, they found LLMs develop modular internal structure where tasks drawing on the same functional network in the human brain recruit overlapping neurons in the model. Their claim is structural — the architecture rhymes. This series' claim is functional — it tests whether the *failure modes* rhyme too, which their paper does not address and does not claim. That distinction is the series' actual contribution, stated explicitly rather than implied: where their paper stops, this series starts.

**The coined term**: **the portability gap** — the space between what a shared cognitive architecture guarantees (the same structure) and what it does not (the same reliability). The series' actual thesis, in one sentence: architecture is portable; correctness is not automatically portable with it, and the gap between the two is exactly where each post's proposition operates.

**The gap is quantified, not left as metaphor** (per external review, matching this blog's "constraint-first" habit of turning a coined term into a computable quantity, the way the Tax series' named taxes each carry a real number): the portability gap for a given property is the latency and compute cost of the secondary, orthogonal verification loop required to audit that property externally, because the property cannot certify itself (the Layer 1 Löb/Critch result from Post 3, generalized at Layer 3 to the other four). An agent that fails a property's ledger entry has a portability gap with a real, computable cost, not just a philosophical asterisk.

## The Property Verdict Ledger (accumulating record artifact)

One row added per post, assembled in full at Post 4's close — same device as the Tax series' Pareto Ledger, adapted to this series' actual deliverable. Columns: Property | Formal Proposition | Human Instance | Agent Instance | Exact vs. Approximate | Verdict (agent demonstrates it / doesn't / contested) | **Control-Plane Consequence**. The ledger *is* the series' running specimen substitute — it accumulates even without a continuous narrative scenario, which answers the one structural gap Theorems Out of Warranty left open (per this blog's own retrospective note: ToW used a fresh example per post and never built a closing bookend — worth doing deliberately here instead of repeating the gap).

**The ledger is a deployment gate, not only a diagnostic** (per external review): a failed verdict on a given property must name the specific architectural response, not stop at "the agent lacks this property." A failed Simulation verdict names a required resource-estimation floor before admission. A failed Awareness verdict names the specific external audit loop required (concretely, this is where H2AI's shadow-mode reconciliation belongs as the worked example — the same mechanism Awareness's own Layer 1 result proves is structurally necessary, not optional). The philosophical apparatus (Putnam, Fisher, Critch, Bandura) exists to justify why the gate is drawn where it is drawn; it does not replace the gate.

## Bookend close

Post 1 opens with the SWE-bench self-assessment case (six frontier LLMs, ~5× token-cost underestimation, per Brainstorm Round 2). Post 4's synthesis returns to that *same* case and reruns it through all five properties and the completed ledger, showing what the fuller framework catches that the Simulation-only reading in Post 1 could not have. This substitutes for a continuous fictional specimen (which the series deliberately doesn't use) while still satisfying the close-the-loop convention every prior multi-part series has used.

**The rerun must trace an explicit causal cascade, not five independent observations about the same case**: the Simulation failure (underestimating required resources) cascades into an Abstraction failure (fixating on a single patch strategy instead of re-abstracting once the first approach's cost assumptions break), which distorts Rationality (discounting test-failure output that disconfirms the chosen approach), which blinds Awareness (unwarranted confidence in a broken diff), which forces Optimization into the iteration trap (escalating retries instead of recognizing a structural ceiling). This is the concrete argument for why compute or retries spent at the Optimization layer cannot fix a deficit that actually originates upstream, and it is what makes the Constraint Sequence Framework citation in the closing synthesis a demonstrated finding rather than an asserted one.

**This cascade maps onto the same MAPE-K loop this blog already formalized in Autonomic Edge Architectures** (per external review, corrected to use all five MAPE-K components rather than four): Monitor ↔ Noticing/Simulation, Analyze ↔ Abstraction, Plan ↔ Rationality, Execute ↔ Optimization, and — this is the finding worth stating explicitly, not just a mnemonic — **Knowledge ↔ Awareness**, since MAPE-K's Knowledge store is a cross-cutting persistent record, not a pipeline stage, exactly the way this series already treats Awareness as standing alone (Post 3) rather than paired like the other four. The series' own post structure and MAPE-K's own architecture turn out to have the same shape; the bookend cascade is where this gets demonstrated on a real case rather than asserted.

## The closing synthesis (Post 4's actual deliverable)

**Corrected by Brainstorm Round 3 — read this section alongside Post 3's row in the structure table above; it is not independent of that correction.** Define a property as **self-consistent** for a system if the system's belief that it possesses that property can be verified using only the system's own internal resources. Post 3's rigorously-scoped result (Critch's generalized Löb's theorem, applied to an agent whose self-trust is modeled as a provability-based reasoner) proves Awareness cannot be fully self-consistent in this sense, for that class of agent — a real Layer 1/Bound claim.

The series closes by proposing that weaker, non-identical versions of the same limitation show up in the other four properties — but this must be stated explicitly as **this series' own structural generalization (Layer 3/Estimate), not as four more instances of literal Gödelian or Löbian proof.** Only Awareness gets an actual formal-system-grade theorem in this series; Simulation, Abstraction, Rationality, and Optimization do not each carry their own hidden formal proof of a self-consistency ceiling, and the post must say so in those words rather than let the parallel structure imply otherwise. What survives at Layer 3 is the argument this makes possible, not a claim of four more theorems: if even a rigorously-scoped formal reasoner cannot fully verify one of its own properties from within, that is a real reason to expect the weaker, informal versions of the same problem elsewhere — and it is the actual argument for why external falsification (evals, shadow-mode data, independent verification) isn't optional scaffolding for any of the five properties. This calls back directly to Theorems Out of Warranty's whole thesis (a guarantee that quietly stops covering what it was never proven to cover) without restating it — the same finding, arrived at from the self-verification-limits direction instead of the borrowed-theorem-warranty direction.

## The unnamed formal pattern (never attributed by name)

Two concepts reused from a real structured inventive-problem-solving methodology, rederived as this series' own original formalization, with no citation or naming of that methodology anywhere, in this document or in the published text:

- **Post 2, Abstraction**: the methodology's contradiction-generalization move (it was built specifically to overcome "psychological inertia," which its own literature identifies as the same phenomenon as functional fixedness — documented history, not analogy) becomes this series' own sufficient-statistic reframing: the antidote to functional fixedness is finding the abstraction that is actually sufficient for the *current* target, not the old one.
- **Post 4, Optimization**: the methodology's ideality concept (benefits → infinity, cost/harm/complexity → zero, rejecting trade-off framing outright) becomes this series' own cost-aware optimal-stopping target: the ideal is the costless idealization; satisficing is the real, correct policy once search cost is priced in, not a suboptimal shortcut (see Resolved Decisions).

## The explicit, cited formal scaffolding

- **Systems theory / cybernetics**: Ashby's Law (Post 1), second-order cybernetics / von Foerster (Post 3).
- **Epistemology**: Popper's falsificationism + Bayesian calibration (Post 2, Rationality).
- **Social cognitive theory**: Bandura's self-efficacy / reciprocal determinism (Post 3, Awareness) — the behavioral-consequence complement to Gödel's logical-limit result in the same post.
- **Game theory**: Nash equilibria vs. Pareto optimality, reward hacking as exploiting a misspecified objective (Post 4) — evidenced with GTBench and the 2025 IJCAI survey "Game Theory Meets Large Language Models."
- **Goodhart's Law, formally split** (Manheim & Garrabrant, 2018 — four distinct mechanisms): Causal Goodhart → Post 2. Regressional Goodhart → Post 3. Extremal + Adversarial Goodhart → Post 4 — evidenced with Krakovna's specification-gaming repository, the 2025 Palisade chess study, and 2025-2026 reward-hacking papers.

## Mandatory Pareto/Constraint-Sequence framing (per this blog's own standing rule)

- **Post 4's satisficing-vs-optimal trade-off gets an explicit achievable-region/Pareto Definition** before Proposition 4 (the optimal-stopping result) uses it — search cost on one axis, distance from the true optimal-stopping value on the other, exactly as this rule requires, not left implicit.
- **The five properties' own dependency order (Simulation → Abstraction → Rationality → Awareness → Optimization, from the founding post's own dependency graph) gets cited explicitly through the real Constraint Sequence Framework** (`@/blog/2025-12-27/index.md#the-constraint-sequence-framework`, already used this way in three 2026-08 posts) when Post 4's synthesis shows that a downstream property's failure cannot be fixed by re-optimizing an upstream one — e.g., an Optimization failure that is actually an unaddressed Awareness gap one level up. This is a real, warranted use of the Framework, not decoration: the series' own findings are a dependency-graph problem.

## Epistemic-honesty typing (Bound / Fit / Estimate, the standard this blog has already adopted)

**Sharpened by Brainstorm Round 1** (panel: philosophy of mind, epistemology, math/logic, game theory, systems theory, probability theory, cognitive science, structured problem-solving methodology; researched against real, current sources before convening — see below). The panel's finding: the series' central claim ("the same formal parameter governs a human brain and a transformer") is not one claim of uniform strength — it is three claims stacked, and only the outermost one carries real philosophical risk. Collapsing them into one statement, as earlier drafts of this spec did, is exactly the kind of overclaim this blog's own epistemic-honesty habit exists to prevent.

- **Layer 1 / Bound (trivially true, no philosophical risk)**: the mathematical theorem itself — Ashby's Law, Gödel/Löb, the sufficient-statistic identity, the optimal-stopping inequality, Manheim & Garrabrant's four Goodhart mechanisms — holds for any information-processing system by construction, regardless of substrate. This is a triviality of the math, not a claim about shared minds, and should be stated plainly without hedging.
- **Layer 2 / Fit (empirically measured, time-bound, two separate measurements)**: the specific human study and the specific agent study each independently found a pattern consistent with the theorem's prediction — the 99.2%/68.95% escalation figures, the functional-fixedness result in LLM-enabled chat search, etc. Stated as two independent findings, not evidence of one shared underlying phenomenon.
- **Layer 3 / Estimate (this series' own interpretive act, the only layer with real risk)**: asserting the human finding and the agent finding are *the same phenomenon* rather than two different phenomena that happen to fit the same formal shape. This is where the classical philosophy-of-mind objection actually lands: multiple realizability (Putnam) is the standard justification for claiming a shared functional property across substrates, but Putnam himself later turned the same argument against functionalism, contending real mental kinds are "compositionally and computationally plastic" — the same kind need not correspond to one clean computational state. That objection targets Layer 3 specifically, and only Layer 3; it does not touch Layer 1 at all. Every property section must name which layer a given sentence is operating at, and Layer 3 claims must cite this exact tension rather than assert identity as settled.
- **Additional caution on the Fedorenko paper specifically**: real, current methodological critique exists of brain-LLM representational-similarity work in general — researchers warn that high alignment scores can result from metric overfitting or shared surface-level features, and that "what brain alignment actually measures" remains contested even within that literature. The series must cite the Fedorenko paper as suggestive Layer 2 evidence consistent with the thesis, never as proof that settles Layer 3.

## Falsification Criteria

Confirmed: yes, one section per post (the Why-X essays' device), stating explicit F1-Fn conditions that would invalidate that post's own claim that the property/paradox pairing holds — e.g., Post 3's Awareness claim would be falsified if a system were shown to verify its own consistency using only internal resources in a case Gödel's theorem should forbid, which would mean the formalization itself, not just the agent, is wrong.

## Register / voice

Formal/theorem-driven register (Tax series, Theorems Out of Warranty), not the essayistic register of the 2024-03 founding post it returns to build on. Full Definition/Proposition/`<details class="proof">` apparatus, Physical Translation blockquotes, real citation footnotes. Standard site-wide prose constraints apply and are non-negotiable for the writing phase: no em-dashes in prose, no comma-stacked subsentences, "where:" bullet lists for variable definitions rather than Term\|Meaning tables, primary/secondary terminology never master/slave, diagrams only where they show something prose cannot. Target length per post: no fewer than 7,500 words, aiming toward the ~20,000-word standard this blog has calibrated against the Tax series' own ~29K average — this is a length floor, not a padding target.

**Per external review, stated as a drafting priority**: the Physical Translation blockquotes carry this blog's actual value proposition and must not be crowded out by the proof apparatus around them. Each one should be the most adversarial, pragmatically dense sentence in its section — the exact reason a production system fails, not a gentler restatement of the theorem in plain words.

## Resolved Decisions

1. **Post 4's satisficing proposition: cost-aware, not cost-free.** Post 4's actual proposition uses a cost-aware search model (Stigler/McCall-style reservation-value results): the satisficing threshold *is* the optimal policy once real search cost is priced in, not a suboptimal approximation of a costless optimum. The ideality target (benefits to infinity, cost to zero) is the costless idealization that real search cost structurally prevents any system, human or agent, from reaching — which is the sharper, more defensible version of the proposition and matches the ideality concept's own definition more precisely than the cost-free framing this spec started with.
